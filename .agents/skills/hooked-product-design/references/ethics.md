# Autonomy and beneficial habits

Book basis: introduction, chapter 4 on autonomy, and chapter 6. Practical safeguards below are extensions; the matrix is the author's reflection tool, not a certification.

## The Manipulation Matrix

The book asks whether the maker would use the product and believes it materially improves users' lives:

| Maker would use it | Believes it improves lives | Book category | Practical question                                             |
| ------------------ | -------------------------- | ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Yes                | Yes                        | Facilitator   | Does direct experience agree with users' outcomes?             |
| No                 | Yes                        | Peddler       | What research compensates for distance from users?             |
| Yes                | No                         | Entertainer   | Is enjoyment chosen and sustainable without harmful pressure?  |
| No                 | No                         | Dealer        | Is the design extracting attention/money without user benefit? |

These categories describe the author's perspective. An AI should not invent personal product usage or diagnose a founder's motives. Ask the responsible humans or mark that dimension unknown. Valuable accessibility, enterprise, and specialist products may be built by people who are not their own users. Research and participatory design matter more than pretending to share the experience.

Entertainment is a legitimate chosen benefit. State it honestly. Do not insist every game must deliver educational, health, or productivity outcomes to deserve improvement.

## Apply proportionate checks

Use a quick benefit/autonomy check for ordinary work; expand when the design creates pressure, sensitive disclosure, spending, or overuse. The skill is not a mandate to block benign edits pending an ethical workshop.

- Does the requested repeated behavior serve a need users endorse?
- Does the product deliver the promised benefit rather than only postponing relief?
- Can the person decline optional work, choose reminders, pause, recover, and leave?
- Could the intervention cause unwelcome exposure, excessive time/spending, shame, or lost trust?
- What observed result would make the team reduce or remove it?

## Replace pressure with useful continuity

| ID    | Problematic design                | Concrete alternative                                                    |
| ----- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ET-01 | Threatening streak-loss copy      | Describe actual state; preserve learning/history; offer a small restart |
| ET-02 | Fake scarcity or social activity  | Show verified capacity or genuine useful content                        |
| ET-03 | Hidden publication or invitations | Explicit audience/recipient choice and deliberate send                  |
| ET-04 | A refusal hidden behind guilt     | Plain visible decline with the promised consequence                     |
| ET-05 | Unbounded chase for rewards       | Useful endpoints, session goals, optional continuation                  |
| ET-06 | Retention through trapped work    | Reliable ownership, retrieval, edit/export/exit                         |
| ET-07 | Re-engagement despite stop/pause  | Respect preferences and suppress nonessential cues                      |

## Overuse and well-being

Habit and harmful compulsive dependency are different; high use alone does not diagnose addiction. The book's numerical statements about pathological use are historical claims, not safe universal thresholds. Do not import them as product limits or reassurance.

Where relevant, consider expressed regret, displacement of intended activities, repeated ignored stopping preferences, and unwanted spending alongside raw time. Use privacy-conscious feedback and user-selected boundaries. Do not create clinical labels from telemetry or claim that a usage cap treats a health condition.

Changes can include reminder caps, quiet periods, opt-in challenge intensity, humane restart, spending transparency, and practical stopping points. Choose measures suited to the actual product and audience instead of adding every control indiscriminately.

## Autonomy copy must match behavior

The book discusses a phrase reaffirming freedom to refuse. Do not present its reported effect size as a conversion guarantee. Write plain choice, provide a usable alternative, and implement it. Saying a choice is optional while blocking the core task contradicts the promise.

If an intervention is unsuitable, explain the actual concern briefly and propose a concrete beneficial alternative. Preserve the original task wherever a safe, useful design can satisfy it.
