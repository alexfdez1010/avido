# Rewards: deliver value and relevant discovery

Book basis: chapter 4. The action catalog and quality checks are practical extensions.

## Satisfy the need before adding variability

The reward must resolve the need that prompted the action. Ask what the user actually receives: a useful answer, social connection, an interesting discovery, or improved competence. Coins, badges, confetti, and points are symbols; they are valuable only when they support a meaningful outcome.

The book distinguishes anticipation from enjoyment and discusses novelty and uncertain rewards. These explanations are design hypotheses, not license to claim a feature releases dopamine or causes a specific neurological response. Current scientific claims need independent verification when consequential.

Keep reliability and novelty separate. The save operation, payment, promised lesson access, and accurate feedback should work predictably. The discovered idea, genuine social reply, or appropriate next challenge can vary. Do not withhold essential results randomly.

## Tribe, hunt, and self

| Type  | Underlying benefit                                | Meaningful variation                                                 | Common mismatch                                     |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Tribe | Connection, recognition, acceptance, contribution | Genuine replies, useful peer feedback, different shared achievements | Forced social comparison or manufactured popularity |
| Hunt  | Useful information, material resources, discovery | Different relevant ideas, search results, worthwhile opportunities   | Random content unrelated to the need                |
| Self  | Competence, mastery, completion, agency           | Appropriate challenge and insight as ability develops                | Badges unrelated to learning or useful completion   |

A product may serve more than one reward type. It does not need all three. Segment by purpose: contributors and consumers can have different motivations in the same product.

## Finite and sustained variability

Finite content becomes known: a solved puzzle set, a completed course, a story ending. Plan refresh costs, a new relevant goal, or graceful completion. Do not relabel the same task and call it novelty.

The book calls ongoing social and user-generated variation infinite variability. This is descriptive shorthand, not literally inexhaustible value. Quality, moderation, relevance, and a healthy community still require work. User-generated content is not a free or automatically better replenishment system.

## Autonomy and reward alignment

Reactance occurs when a person feels their freedom is threatened. Offer meaningful choices and support familiar desired activities. A required logging chore can undermine a benefit even if its metrics look precise. The ability to refuse must be real; a polite sentence does not neutralize a coercive flow.

Social learning examples in the book suggest people may learn from relatable peers. Show genuine, relevant examples of helpful behavior without fabricated testimonials or compulsory disclosure.

## Reward review

Ask users what they found satisfying, encouraging, surprising, or disappointing. Verify the useful result with behavior where possible. Check whether desire to return comes from relevant continuing value or from an unresolved task deliberately kept incomplete.
