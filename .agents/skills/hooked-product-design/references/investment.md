# Investment: make the next experience better

Book basis: chapter 5. Persistence, permission, and evaluation details are practical extensions.

## Action versus investment

Action seeks today's anticipated reward; investment contributes time, effort, data, content, skill, money, or social value for future usefulness. The same interaction can perform both roles, but identify each separately. Paying is not automatically useful investment; clicking a compulsory extra screen is not stored value.

Default to a small investment after experienced value. Some products necessarily require initial input to deliver value; explain why, minimize the ask, and distinguish required setup from optional future enrichment. The ordering is a design principle, not a prohibition on essential input.

Two questions establish investment quality: **What is stored or improved? What future need or event becomes easier to meet?** It may also prepare a relevant next cue. Never add artificial work to create attachment.

## Five forms of stored value

| Form in the book | Useful example                         | Future benefit                        | Design concern                  |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Content          | Saved item, annotation, created work   | Retrieve or build on it               | Persistence and export          |
| Data             | Chosen preference, organized record    | Relevant result with less setup       | Necessity, correction, privacy  |
| Followers        | Curated relationships/audience         | Relevant connection and communication | Consent and social exposure     |
| Reputation       | Genuine history of contribution        | Trust and recognition                 | Accuracy, fairness, correction  |
| Skill            | Learned interaction or real capability | Easier action, deeper challenge       | Transferable benefit and access |

History, progress, and plans can combine these forms. State exactly which future benefit exists instead of calling every collected field personalization.

## Load a next trigger

Specify a causal path: saving a goal allows a chosen reminder; asking a question allows a real reply alert; following a topic allows relevant new-content discovery. Define eligibility, timing, channel permission, suppression, destination, and user controls. A generic daily notification unrelated to invested value is not evidence of a completed loop.

Shorter cycle time matters only when the next need occurs sooner. Do not accelerate reminders ahead of the user's purpose to manufacture frequency. Ask whether the next visit is easier, more relevant, or more useful than the previous one.

## Psychological mechanisms and limits

The book discusses valuing one's labor (IKEA effect), consistency with prior commitments, cognitive dissonance/rationalization, and reciprocity after help. Treat these as explanations to investigate. They do not make every effort valuable or justify escalating burdens and sunk-cost pressure.

Support a user's chosen commitment through useful continuity. Avoid implying they must continue because of time or money already spent. Do not break familiar interactions solely to force new learning and increase switching cost.

## Investment handoff

Describe the ask, why now, saved value, persistence and failure behavior, next benefit, optional next cue, edit/undo route, and success metric. Specify account/offline/cross-device behavior only where relevant and supported; do not promise synchronization that has not been implemented.
